2Why I built it

A project, and a test.

This started as a personal project to put the things I care about in one place: markets, sports and music. I work in investor relations, so I read ownership data, earnings and market moves all day, and I follow sports closely outside of work.

It was also a test. I wanted to see how far AI could go when I handed it a real build instead of a demo: a working site with regularly refreshed data, automatic checks and a track record I can’t quietly rewrite. My job was deciding what to build, setting the rules, and cutting what didn’t hold up.

The bigger question for me is how AI changes the way I work, live and play. So far: it’s faster than I expected, it makes confident mistakes, and the value is in knowing what to ask and how to check the answer. This site is where I’m working that out in public.

4How far to trust it

  • Every number has a source.SEC, FRED, ESPN and Nasdaq among them. Under the hood lists where each one comes from and how often it refreshes.
  • Bad data is held back.Files that fail an automatic check are not published, so a number is blank rather than wrong.
  • The sports model shows its misses.Including the steps that did nothing. See the model history.
  • Know the limits.Prices are delayed, 13F data lags a quarter, and nothing here is investment or betting advice.

6Who did what

What I did

My input and my actions

  • Picked the idea and the scope. One site for markets, sports and music, then which pages each section needed and which to cut.
  • Set the look. A terminal feel on black, and a separate accent color for each section.
  • Set the rules. Nothing hardcoded to me or my teams, no betting lines for now, and a number that can’t be verified is shown as N/A instead of guessed.
  • Opened the accounts it runs on. The GitHub repo, Cloudflare hosting, the nathanskown.dev domain and the data-source keys.
  • Reviewed everything and pushed back. I read each page, flagged what looked off or confusing, and approved the wording before it went public.
  • Decided what ships and when. Music stayed unpublished until every piece was connected, and market commentary is rule-based so the site doesn’t depend on ongoing AI usage.

What AI drove

The building

  • Wrote all of the code. The pages, styling and animation, plus the scripts that pull from SEC filings, FRED, ESPN and Nasdaq.
  • Built the sports model. Team ratings from past games, 10,000-run season simulations and a public log of every pick, graded after the final.
  • Runs the data pipeline. Scheduled refresh jobs, and automatic checks that hold back a file that fails them.
  • Proposed ideas for me to accept or cut. Some features, like the song-guessing games, started as its suggestions.
  • Drafted the copy. Page descriptions and notes, which I then edited.
  • Did the repetitive work. Applying one fix across every page, then committing and deploying it.

AI does the building. I do the deciding and the checking.It also made confident mistakes along the way: figures it couldn’t back up and features that looked good but meant nothing. Catching and cutting those was my job.
