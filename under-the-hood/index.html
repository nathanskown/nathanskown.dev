How a number gets to your screen

1 · SourcesPublic feeds only: SEC EDGAR, FRED, Yahoo Finance, Nasdaq, Kalshi, ESPN, MLB Stats API, MoneyPuck, nflverse, Deezer, Last.fm.
2 · Scheduled jobsGitHub Actions fetch on a timetable: markets hourly in trading hours, sports hourly while games are on, filings daily, 13F quarterly.
3 · Publishing gateEvery file is checked before it is saved: prices in sane ranges, odds that add to 100%, records that add up. A failing file is held back.
4 · EdgeFiles are served from Cloudflare's network. A small server answers live requests: any US ticker, live scoreboards, the music leaderboard.
5 · Your browserPages draw charts, odds and projections from those files. Times show in your own time zone.

No paid data, no scraping of pages whose terms forbid it, and no betting lines shown anywhere. Market prices are delayed about 15 minutes. Nothing here is investment advice.

Is the sports model any good?

The house model rates every team from game results alone, then projects each game before it starts. Every pick is saved before kickoff and graded after the final; none is back-filled. This is the live record so far.

Calibration: when the model gave a team a given chance, how often that team actually won. A well-calibrated model sits on the diagonal. Small buckets are noisy.

Settings are fit on past seasons and kept only if they beat the simple version on later seasons the fit never saw. "Better than simple" is how much lower the prediction error (log loss) was on those unseen seasons than a plain rating with no tuning.
