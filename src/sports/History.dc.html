Model history

The house model went live on October 14 with one season of results and guessed settings. Since then it has been given more history, last season’s ratings, fitted settings, rest days, expected goals and starting pitchers. This page replays the past seasons with each ingredient added in turn, and scores every step on games the settings were never fitted on, so you can see what each one was worth, including the ones that were worth nothing.

What this page does not prove

  • It is a replay, run after the fact. The “launch” rows re-run the first model’s method (one season of results, a prior pulled toward average, no history) with the same engine as the later versions. That is close to what went live but not byte-identical; MLB in particular launched on season totals from the standings.
  • Only four seasons per league are truly out of sample. Older seasons in the chart were part of the data today’s settings were fitted on. They are shown hollow for that reason.
  • Several steps did nothing. Where a step moved Brier by less than .0005 it is labeled as no real change, and a few moved it slightly the wrong way. The tuner keeps a setting only if the whole bundle wins across the held-out seasons, not each piece alone.
  • The big wins are in college sports; the pros are close to a ceiling. The gains for the NFL, NHL, NBA and MLB are small because single games in those leagues are noisy and public ratings are already close to what scores alone can say. Do not read a .002 gain as a different model.
  • It has barely been tested live. The live record above is still short; it began on October 14.
